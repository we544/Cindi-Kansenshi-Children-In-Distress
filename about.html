Who We Are

CINDI Kansenshi is a community-based organisation in Nkwazi compound, Ndola, working with orphaned and vulnerable children and their families.

Our Story

Born from the Community

CINDI Kansenshi stands for Children in Distress. It was founded in 1994 by four ladies concerned for the plight of orphans and vulnerable children in Nkwazi. They used to meet under a tree in the community and work out how to help the children who approached them.

It was at the start of the AIDS pandemic, and many children were caring for ill and dying parents, with no money for food or school fees. With the advent of ARVs, the HIV story has changed, but poverty, suffering, and need remain. The challenges within our communities are still much the same 30 years later.

Today, CINDI works with families, schools, and community members to help children grow up healthy, happy, and self-supporting. We support education, nutrition, health, farming, and skills training so that children can thrive within their own communities.

Our Mission

To assist and support children within their extended or adopted families, in their own communities, so they can grow up healthy, happy, and self-supporting members of society.

Our Vision

A future where children in Nkwazi have access to education, nutrition, health support, and the tools to become resilient leaders in their own communities.

Our Values

  • Dignity — every child is treated with respect
  • Transparency — open books, honest reporting
  • Community — decisions made with locals, not for them
  • Compassion — we lead with love

Stories of Change

"Before CINDI, I used to go to school hungry. Now I get a meal every day and I have all my books. I want to become a doctor so I can come back and help my community."

— Grace M., age 13

"They trained me to make and sell soap. Now I earn enough to keep my three grandchildren in school. I never thought at 68 I would have my own small business."

— Grandmother Esther C., caregiver

"The children who come through CINDI arrive fed, with materials, and ready to learn. The difference in concentration and results is night and day."

— Mr. Njovu, class teacher, Kansenshi Primary
