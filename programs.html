Education Support

Keeping Children in School

CINDI Community School has 7 classrooms and 9 teachers. It serves approximately 400 pupils from grades 1 to 7, including children from the local community. Government provides a grant each term to help run the school.

Each pupil receives a cup of soy porridge in the morning, and the Government is currently supporting a lunch programme. CINDI also supports 63 secondary learners in grades 8 to 12 at different schools. Families contribute what they can toward uniforms and books, while child-headed households receive support with these essentials.

Learning for the Future

Computer Lessons and Further Education

Camara, an Irish charity supporting IT learning in vulnerable communities, donated 10 computers to the school's lab and trained CINDI teachers and staff. Educational games and learning programmes run on the computers without internet access. A CINDI student from a child-headed household who studies IT at nearby Zamtel College teaches fellow students on Saturdays, helping them prepare for college while supporting his household.

CINDI supports students in third-level and skills training programmes according to their circumstances. Support can include fees, accommodation, food, clothing, a laptop, and a smartphone. Older students mentor new students as they adjust to further education. The list below reproduces the current student information supplied by CINDI.

Nutrition & Feeding

Food Support for Families

Thirty-six families are registered for nutritional support. Each receives two gallons of mealie meal, a packet of beans, a packet of salt, and one bar of washing soap. Five child-headed families receive a full 25kg bag of mealie meal, along with the other foodstuffs.

At the community school, each child receives a cup of soy porridge in the morning, and the Government is currently supporting a lunch programme. CINDI's farm also gives families an opportunity to grow food with the organisation's support.

Health & Wellness

Healthy Children, Brighter Futures

We support families through health education, simple skills training, medical support, and practical care. Our work recognises that children who are unwell, traumatised, or unsupported often struggle to learn and participate in their communities.

Alongside family support, CINDI offers health education and medical support. The organisation was founded during the AIDS pandemic, when children were caring for ill and dying parents; although the HIV story has changed with ARVs, poverty and the needs of affected families remain.

Community Empowerment

Growing Food, Skills, and Stability

Chief Chiwala gave CINDI a 50-hectare farm where families can grow their own food with CINDI's support. It is about 23 km from CINDI by road, or approximately 8 km by bush paths, including a river crossing.

Sixty-seven families are on the CINDI farm list. Last year, 40 families cultivated there; 35 of those farmers had previously received CINDI support and were weaned off the programme. Some continue to receive education support while farming, while others now farm without household support. The 67 guardians have formed a co-operative to improve their livelihoods.
