Education Support

Keeping Children in School

CINDI supports children in their school journey by covering essential costs and helping families stay engaged with learning. Our community school in Nkwazi has 7 classrooms and is open to children from grade 1 to 7, with approximately 400 pupils currently enrolled.

We also support 63 secondary school learners from grades 8 to 12, with families contributing what they can toward uniforms and books. This model helps children remain in school while keeping families involved and dignified.

Nutrition & Feeding

Food Support for Families

Under our nutritional support programme, 36 families receive food essentials including two gallons of mealie meal, beans, salt, and washing soap. Five child-headed households receive a full 25kg bag of mealie meal alongside the other foodstuffs.

We also support the CINDI farm initiative, where families work together to grow food and build longer-term food security. This helps reduce hunger while preserving dignity and community responsibility.

Health & Wellness

Healthy Children, Brighter Futures

We support families through health education, simple skills training, medical support, and practical care. Our work recognises that children who are unwell, traumatised, or unsupported often struggle to learn and participate in their communities.

In addition to nutrition and family support, CINDI helps with child health needs and connects families to services, with a strong focus on HIV awareness, care, and psychosocial support.

Health education and community support
Medical and nutritional care coordination
HIV awareness and care support
Emotional and social support for vulnerable children
Partner on Health
Community Empowerment

Growing Food, Skills, and Stability

CINDI supports families through practical livelihood work, including a 50-hectare farm given by Chief Chiwala. This farm gives families the opportunity to grow food, build stronger households, and move toward long-term resilience.

There are currently 67 families on the farm list, and the community has formed a co-operative so they can work together and improve their livelihoods. This is a key pillar of CINDI's approach to dignity, self-reliance, and lasting support.
