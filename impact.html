Our Journey

From a Tree to a Community Response

1994

Founded by Concerned Women

Four local women begin meeting under a tree in Nkwazi to care for orphaned and vulnerable children who were going hungry and missing school.

Early years

Food, care, and practical support

CINDI responds to the crisis of AIDS, poverty, and child-headed households by helping children access food, schooling, and stable community care.

Today

Community School and Family Support

Our school serves around 400 pupils in grades 1–7, with 63 secondary learners supported, plus food packages and farm-based livelihoods for families.

Current Programme Reach

Practical Support, From School to Livelihoods

7 classrooms

At CINDI Community School, serving approximately 400 pupils in grades 1–7 with 9 teachers.

School meals

Each pupil receives a cup of soy porridge in the morning; Government currently supports a lunch programme.

36 families

Registered for food support, including mealie meal, beans, salt, and washing soap.

5 child-headed families

Receive a full 25kg bag of mealie meal alongside the other foodstuffs.

50-hectare farm

Given to CINDI by Chief Chiwala; 67 families are on the farm list and guardians have formed a co-operative.

10 computers

Donated through Camara for the school computer lab, with Saturday lessons led by a CINDI IT student.

Looking Ahead

Education Beyond Secondary School

CINDI supports 63 secondary learners in grades 8–12 and is increasingly focused on helping young people reach third-level education and skills training. Support varies by student and may include fees, accommodation, food, clothing, a laptop, and a smartphone. Older students help mentor new students through the challenges of their first year.

The current list of 33 third-level and skills-training students, including institutions, courses, year or waiting-list status, and support, is available on the Education programme page.
